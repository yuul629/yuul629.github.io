## Project description
In this project a interactive gardening experience backed by a deep learning (DL) model was developed. The concept, model and the visual designs were developed in a compressed time line of 1.5 weeks.

In the game, everything revolves around gardening flowers. The game guides users through the whole gardening process through 5 different touch gestures that sybolized steps you take when growing the flow: digging in the earth, planting a seed, covering the seed, watering the seed and finally picking the flow you have grown. 
There are also special challenges, like watering the plant just right. If you give too much water, the flower will wilt, and this is shown directly on the screen. The player also receives feedback on whether the gesture was correctly recognized by the model, so they can try again if needed.

### My contributions
During this project I colleborated with a fellow student, together we developed the concept and defined the animations. My main responsibility was the development of the AI based gesture recognition, including data preparation, model architecture, training, and evaluation.

## Engineering decisions

### Preprocessing of the data
The data was augmented by adding random noise to it to increase the size of the dataset, effectively tripling the dataset. Augmenting the data enabled us to train a more robust model with higher accuracy.  Then labels were encoded into a numerical integer to be able to train the LSTM. 
The data was then split randomly into training and testing data. In this split 30 percent of the data was used for testing. From the testing data, 30 percent of the data was randomly split into validation data to be able to validate the model. Then the data was split into batches of 32, and the data that did not fit into a batch was dropped. 

### Handeling variable-length gesture data
As the gesture data consisted of temporal sequences with varying lengths. I selected a LSTM based architecture, as it could model temporal dependencies while handeling variable-length motion sequences.

A added a bidrectional LSTM so that the model could learn patters from both earlier and later gestures.This increased accuracy by approximately 0.10. The limited dataset introduced the risk of overfitting. To mittigate the risk, dropout and L2 regularization were introduced in combination with the AdamW optimizer. This resulted in a model architecture designed specifically around the temporal nature and limited scale of the available data.

## Outcome
### Model

The select model to be used for the game had an accuracy of 0.8749 and the training loss was 0.4232.
