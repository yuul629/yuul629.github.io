<h2 id="project-description">Project description</h2>
This project explored the design of an interactive feedback system to help users improve their deadlift form. A side-view camera captures the user’s movement, which is displayed on a screen with visual overlays indicating correct and incorrect posture. Surrounding LEDs provide an additional attention cue when incorrect form is detected.

The concept was developed to address injury risk during strength training. Research indicates that young male gym users, particularly those between 18 and 25 years old, are at increased risk of lower-back injuries [1]. The deadlift was selected because incorrect execution can place substantial load on the lower back, while correct performance depends strongly on posture and movement quality. This makes it well suited to real-time visual feedback.

The resulting system provides a low-cost and accessible way for users to receive immediate feedback on their technique, particularly when training without a coach or training partner.

### My contributions

I was responsible for developing the software that evaluated the user's lifting form. I implemented a rule-based evaluation system that translated movement data into real-time feedback, allowing the prototype to identify incorrect execution without relying on machine learning.

<h2 id="engineering-decisions">Engineering decisions</h2>

### Rule-based approach

During development, I implemented a rule-based approach to evaluate deadlift form. This was selected because the project required a transparent and controllable system that could be developed with limited data and adjusted quickly during prototyping. Instead of training a machine-learning model, I translated observed movement patterns from reference videos into explicit evaluation criteria.

### Joint-geometry evaluation

To track body position during the deadlift, I used MediaPipe to extract a limited set of body landmarks from the camera feed. I selected the ankle, knee, hip, shoulder, and ear because these points provided enough information to evaluate the user’s posture while keeping the computational requirements low.

MediaPipe was chosen because it provided a reliable and accessible way to obtain body-joint coordinates in real time without developing a custom pose-estimation model.

### Callibration of the system

To calibrate the system, I used videos of experienced lifters demonstrating correct technique. I then tested the resulting rules with fellow students who had prior gym experience and no history of injury during the exercise, using their performance to assess whether the system could distinguish between correct and incorrect form.

<h2 id="design-decisions">Design decisions</h2>

### Research based approach
After reviewing several directions with the project mentors, we selected a gym installation as the most viable concept. It offered the strongest opportunity for direct interaction while remaining realistic to develop within the available project timeline.

The concept focused on providing real-time feedback during a deadlift. A side-view camera captures the user’s movement, while a screen positioned in front of the user displays their posture together with corrective visual overlays. This setup was chosen to make feedback available during the movement itself, rather than only after completion.

### Visual feedback overlay

The first low-fidelity prototype used coloured lines to compare the user’s current posture with an ideal deadlift posture. Prototype testing showed that participants could understand the concept, but the feedback was not immediately clear without additional context.

One recurring issue was the use of red to represent the user’s current body position. Participants associated red with an error state, which made the feedback harder to interpret. Based on this evaluation, I changed the current-position overlay from red to blue, while retaining green for the target posture.

The prototype was also revised so that the target posture could update throughout different phases of the deadlift instead of representing only a limited number of static positions.

### LED attention cue

The visual overlay provided detailed corrective information, but it required the user to actively look at the screen. To make incorrect posture more noticeable during the movement, LEDs were added around the display as a secondary feedback channel.

The LEDs activate whenever the user moves outside the acceptable posture range. Their purpose is not to explain the correction itself, but to attract the user’s attention to the screen, where the more detailed visual feedback is displayed.

<h2 id = "outcome">Outcome</h2>

### Functional prototype

The project resulted in a functional prototype that could track the user’s deadlift posture, compare it against predefined movement criteria, and provide real-time visual and LED feedback.

### User evaluation

The evaluation showed that participants generally understood the purpose of the system and considered the feedback useful for improving their form. The instruction video improved initial understanding, while the testing also identified clear areas for refinement, particularly the colour and brightness of the LEDs and the meaning of the visual feedback lines.

### Project outcome

The concept demonstrated potential as an accessible training aid, particularly for less experienced gym users. The project was also awarded Most Viable Start-up Concept, highlighting its perceived potential beyond the prototype stage.
